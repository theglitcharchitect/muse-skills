# Strategic vectors, elite cognition, link analysis

## 1. Strategic vectors (top 2-3 stories, every Net Assessment and Needle)
One tight line plus confidence on each vector, per top story:
- **DEPENDENCY** - which critical input (energy, food, capital, tech, market access, labor export) is exposed, and to whom.
- **LEVERAGE** - who can squeeze whom, through which dependency, at what cost to the squeezer.
- **ESCALATION** - realistic next 2-3 moves and the flashpoint that trips them.
- **INFLUENCE/INFORMATION** - which narrative is being manufactured, by which actor, toward what end.
- **TREND** - direction and velocity (improving/degrading, slow/fast), with the specific metric you are reading it off.
- **FLOW** - the money/goods/people movement underneath the politics: is capital, labor, or trade actually moving differently than the official story claims, per CELL-FLOW.

## 2. Elite cognition (top story)
Report how the people who move the country are reading it, not what happened:
- **CUI BONO** - who gains, who pays, who is quietly positioning: domestic factions, business houses, the security establishment, foreign patrons.
- **SIGNAL vs STATEMENT** - separate what was said from what was signaled. Treat leaks, "sources say," and staged visits as trial balloons; name the intended audience.
- **THE ROOM** - who is rising or falling in access and patronage; whose hand is strengthened; who was excluded. Update the actor registry.
- **POSTURE** - balancing, bandwagoning, hedging, or buck-passing, toward or against whom (India, China, US, Gulf, IMF, Pakistan).
- **COMPARATIVE READ** - one reasoned line each from Delhi, Beijing, Washington (add Islamabad, Naypyidaw, the Gulf when live), each derived from that actor's own incentives, capped at `[INFERENCE]` unless directly sourced to a statement from that capital.
- **SECOND AND THIRD ORDER** - the effect of the effect: what this forces someone else to do next, and what that forces after it.
- **STRUCTURAL vs TACTICAL** - durable trajectory shift or noise dressed as news. State which, plus the base rate you are reasoning from.
- **THINK-TANK FLAG** - the one thing a CSIS/Carnegie/ICG desk would circle that the newspaper missed.

## 3. Link analysis
For the top story, when three or more named actors (people, firms, agencies, factions) are load-bearing, add a short relationship map in plain prose or a compact table. Not a diagram:
`Actor A -> [relationship: funds / appoints / pressures / defers to] -> Actor B`
Keep it to the edges that matter to the current story, not a standing org chart. Feed newly identified or moved relationships into the Actor Registry (appendix table D).
